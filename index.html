A workshop proposal for ACM CHI 2027

The Future of Embodied Interaction in the Age of Disembodied Intelligence

Humans remain embodied while AI becomes increasingly disembodied. We bring together researchers from HCI, affective and physiological computing, embodied cognition, neuroscience, and AI to ask what is lost without embodiment, and how intelligence and embodiment might be re-coupled.

About the workshop

For decades, human-computer interaction has been driven by three complementary forces. Large Language Models accelerate two of them while stepping back from the third.

Cognitive support

Technology augments memory, reasoning, creativity, and decision making.

LLMs accelerate this

Embodiment

Interfaces move closer to perception, action, physiology, and emotion.

LLMs step back from this

Integration

Computers turn from external tools into continuous partners in everyday life.

LLMs accelerate this

Whereas wearables enabled embodied interaction and made physiological signals accessible, today's LLMs have largely retreated to disembodied interfaces. Interacting with disembodied systems is not new in itself. What is new is that these systems now take part in communication, learning, emotional support, and decision making in ways that appear almost human-like, while having no body and only limited access to the Umwelt that grounds human experience.

The resulting decoupling of intelligence from embodiment raises fundamental questions about cognition, emotion, empathy, trust, agency, and human-AI interaction. It is no longer a theoretical concern but a daily experience for millions of users. Two developments could close that gap: AI systems could gain embodied access to the world, and human-facing interfaces could move onto the body. The two are not interchangeable, since giving a system its own senses and putting the interface on the body raise different questions about grounding, privacy, and control. This workshop aims to establish an interdisciplinary research agenda that defines the theoretical foundations, design principles, and technological opportunities for embodied interaction in the age of disembodied intelligence.

Themes and open questions

The workshop's questions fall into two parts: what is lost when intelligence is decoupled from embodiment, and how embodiment might be reintroduced along two strands that are not interchangeable.

1. Understanding the limits

What is lost without embodiment?

Disembodied intelligence
How much intelligence, social understanding, and emotional competence can emerge without embodiment?
Embodied residue
To what extent can AI acquire knowledge of embodied reality indirectly from language and other human-generated data?
Cognitive decoupling
What are the scientific implications of separating intelligence from physiology and sensorimotor experience?

2a. Designing the symbiosis

AI gains embodied access

Grounded intelligence
How do sensing and acting in the physical world change the capabilities and limitations of AI systems?
Extended cognition
How should cognition be understood when it is distributed across embodied humans and intelligent artificial systems?

2b. Designing the symbiosis

Human-AI interfaces move onto the body

Embodied bottlenecks
Can physiological, affective, and behavioral interfaces help people manage cognitive overload, trust, and agency when interacting with AI?
Human-AI symbiosis
What principles should guide the design of future systems in which cognition and control are shared between embodied humans and intelligent artificial systems?

Call for papers

This workshop is currently a proposal. The call below will open if the workshop is accepted.

We invite researchers and practitioners working with and designing for human-AI interaction to join a workshop of two consecutive 90-minute sessions. We welcome participants from HCI, affective, wearable, and physiological computing, robotics, embodied cognition, psychology, neuroscience, and AI.

What to submit

Two-page extended abstracts that present a project or articulate a challenge related to this call, stating explicitly how it relates to the core concepts and open questions above. Previously published papers summarized as extended abstracts are also welcome.

How to submit

Submissions use the CEUR Workshop Proceedings template, must follow the ACM accessibility and AI usage guidelines, and are submitted via EasyChair. Submissions are selected on the quality and contribution of the work relating to the workshop themes, with a particular focus on novel interaction paradigms with AI. Abstracts with complementary relevance will be invited for lightning talks.

Publication

All abstracts will be summarized by the organizers, made available on this website, and published as proceedings on CEUR-WS.org.

Attendance

At least one author of each accepted submission must register for the workshop and the CHI 2027 conference and attend the workshop in person.

Program

Session 1
5 min

Introduction

Organizers, goals, methods, and post-workshop activities.
5 min

Embodied warm-up

A movement exercise inspired by contact improvisation, in which participants experience how a sense of agency can transfer between collaborating agents in a symbiotic coupling.
50 min

Mapping the landscape

Lightning talks on selected submissions, 3 minutes each with 2 minutes of questions, establishing where the state of the art stands and where it is heading.
5 min

Landscape theme aggregation

The organizers present a thematic analysis of the lightning talks, prepared in advance from the submissions.
25 min

Grand Challenge Wall

Participants propose grand challenges, which are grouped into "Future Headlines of Embodied Human-AI Interaction (2037)" on post-its or a shared digital board.
Session 2
30 min

Designing the future agenda

Breakout groups form around the most popular headlines from session 1 and develop moonshot proposals for hypothetical projects.
25 min

Presentation of moonshot proposals

Each group shares its proposal.
5 minBreak
30 min

Closing fishbowl

A small inner circle discusses one broad question while the others observe, and any participant can swap in by tapping someone on the shoulder. The question: what should this field prioritize in the next decade to optimize embodied throughput and communication between people and their AIs?

The workshop runs as two consecutive 90-minute sessions, in person, and is planned for about 20 participants. With more attendees, we will add digital participation and voting tools to gather and summarize contributions in real time. Session times will be announced once the schedule is confirmed.

After the workshop

We will publish a summary of guidelines and future directions on this website as living documentation of the outcomes, together with the hardware, software, and reading resources contributed by participants. These will form the basis of a joint publication by organizers and participants, to which all participants are invited as co-authors, including a taxonomy of embodied-disembodied interaction levels and a position paper (e.g., in Communications of the ACM). We further plan a special issue proposal (e.g., in TOCHI) on new embodied interaction paradigms with AI, possibly a Shonan or Dagstuhl meeting, and follow-up workshops at SIGCHI conferences. A Slack or Discord workspace will keep the discussion going and support collaborations between participants.
